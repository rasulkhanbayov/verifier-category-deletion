A. FOR EVERY SUBMISSION
================================================================================

A1. Did you describe the limitations of your work?
    YES — "Limitations", placed after the Conclusion and before the references,
    excluded from the page count as ARR requires. It covers: language coverage
    (all three tasks are English) with the typological prediction that follows;
    what the ceiling does and does not bound, including that the reported
    figures evaluate Cor. 3(b) under a homogeneity assumption that is false in
    practice and understates the heterogeneous case; the A2 conditional-
    independence failure for stacks sharing a parse; contamination; and the
    absence of a total compute figure.

A2. Did you discuss any potential risks of your work?
    YES — "Ethical considerations". The specific risk named is that the floor of
    Prop. 1 is a guarantee about expected accuracy and must not be presented to
    downstream users as a correctness guarantee, since a system that never
    degrades on average can still fail badly on an individual input.

A3. Do the abstract and introduction summarize the paper's main claims?
    YES.

A4. Have you used AI assistants in writing, and is that disclosed?
    See E1. Answer there, not here.

C. DID YOU RUN COMPUTATIONAL EXPERIMENTS?   YES
================================================================================

C1. Did you report the number of parameters, compute budget, and infrastructure?
    PARTIALLY, AND THIS MUST BE ANSWERED AS "PARTIALLY".
    Infrastructure: yes — OpenRouter-hosted inference for the four API models,
    plus one local GPU (Tesla P100) for the constrained-decoding reference
    point. Parameters: model sizes are stated where public (8B, 70B); the OpenAI
    models' parameter counts are not disclosed by the provider.
    Compute budget: NO. Total spend was not tracked per experiment from the

    Limitations rather than substituting an estimate.
    >> Do NOT answer "yes" here. The paper openly says the figure does not
       exist; a "yes" would be exactly the misleading filing ARR desk-rejects.

C2. Did you discuss the experimental setup, including hyperparameter search?
    YES — greedy decoding for the base output, temperature 0.7 for candidates,
    N in {1,2,4,8,16,32}, seed 0, 200 instances per cell. No hyperparameter
    search was run: the decoding settings were fixed in advance and not tuned,
    and the trained scorers are logistic regressions over fixed feature sets.
    >> AUTHOR ACTION (B9): record the OpenRouter model snapshot strings and
       access dates. These are moving endpoints and no snapshot is recorded
       anywhere in the bundle. Without them the setup is not reproducible, and
       C2 and D-series reproducibility answers are weaker than they look.

C3. Did you report descriptive statistics, e.g. error bars?
    PARTIALLY, AND THIS MUST BE ANSWERED AS "PARTIALLY".
    Reported: paired bootstrap 95% confidence intervals over instances, a paired
    permutation test, and the minimum detectable effect (7.5-9.3 accuracy points
    at 80% power) stated in Section 6 BEFORE any near-null is reported.
    Not reported: variation across random seeds. Every cell is a single draw at
    seed 0, and no standard deviations across seeds appear anywhere. Section 6
    does not claim otherwise; an earlier draft did and the claim was removed.
    >> This is blocking finding B4. Answer "partially" and point to Limitations.

C4. Did you report the packages used?
    YES — sqlglot for SQL parsing, transformers and outlines for the local
    constrained-decoding run, numpy for the analysis. Appendix A and the
    supplementary archive.
    >> AUTHOR ACTION: add version numbers for each. Package versions matter for

D. DID YOU USE HUMAN ANNOTATORS OR RESEARCH WITH HUMAN PARTICIPANTS?   NO
================================================================================

    Answer NO for this submission, and answer it plainly.

    Appendix C designs a human validation of the correctness label (V1: 150
    epsilon events per task, two annotators blind to the automatic label and to
    each other, third adjudicating, Krippendorff's alpha reported with a
    pre-registered floor of 0.67). It has NOT been run. A design is not an
    annotation study, and answering "yes" on the strength of a design would be
    a misleading filing.

    >> If V1 is run before the deadline, D1-D5 all become answerable and must be
       completed: instructions given to participants, recruitment and payment,
       consent, ethics-board approval, and annotator demographics.

THE ONE JUDGEMENT CALL A REVIEWER COULD GO EITHER WAY ON
================================================================================

  ARR warns: "Including key materials in appendices could be viewed as an
  attempt to circumvent the content page count, and result in desk rejection."

  This paper reached 8 pages partly by moving material to the appendix: the full
  rate table, the main comparison table, Figure 2, the protocol, the check
  definitions and the validity designs. Proofs, protocol and check definitions
  are explicitly permitted there. The two result tables are the judgement call.

  The argument that it is compliant: the main body is self-contained for every
  claim. Table 1 gives p_0, epsilon and the ceiling for all seven certified
  cells, which is the headline. Section 7 states the E3 result (24/24 SQL, 4/4
  IE cells), the two composition cells (71.5->73.5 and 43.0->45.5), and the E3b
  null (p >= 0.112) in the body text. A reviewer can assess every claim without
  turning to the appendix; the appendix tables carry the full supporting data.

  The residual risk, stated plainly: a reviewer who feels the full comparison
  table belongs in the body may say so. If you want to remove that risk, bring
  Table 6 (the measured rates) into the main paper. It needs roughly 0.35 of a
  page, and Related Work is the place to find it: it currently runs 0.95 pages
  and would still work at 0.65. That is a trade, not a fix, and it is yours to
  make rather than ours.
