WHAT THE SECOND SEARCH CHANGED

Round 1 killed the medical-imaging framing. Round 2 nearly killed the
replacement, and forced a second reposition. Both were the right outcome.

  FOUND  arXiv:2507.12399, "ROC-n-reroll" (Dorner, Chen, Cruz, Yang).
         Proves the accuracy of rejection sampling and best-of-N is
         characterised by the geometry of the verifier's ROC curve. This is
         MORE GENERAL than the previous draft's Prop. 2, which was a single
         point on that curve presented as the contribution.
  EFFECT Prop. 2 is no longer a contribution. It is now presented as the
         sound corner (TPR = 1) of their framework, cited as such. What
         survives as ours: the GATE. Their two rules are ungated and cannot
         have a floor, because ungated selection can discard a correct base
         output at any epsilon. Props. 1, 4 and Cor. 1 all depend on the
         gate and have no analogue in their analysis.

  FOUND  arXiv:2505.14625 (TinyV): 38.5% of responses a rule-based math
         verifier REJECTED were in fact correct. arXiv:2510.00915: rule-based
         checkers are systematically brittle to formatting and algebraic
         equivalence.
  EFFECT This is the most valuable finding of the search. It contradicts
         the naive form of the soundness claim and became the paper's second
         axis: NOMINAL soundness (the task argument) versus REALISED
         soundness (the measured delta). The floor depends on the latter.
         The gap between them is where practitioners lose a guarantee they
         believe they have. New Definition 1, new Experiment 7, new
         prediction P2.

  FOUND  arXiv:2506.18203 (Weaver): ensembles weak verifiers by a learned
         WEIGHTED score.
  EFFECT Narrows Prop. 4 but does not kill it. A weighted score cannot
         preserve delta = 0, since any positive weight on an unsound member
         can outvote a sound one. Conjunction preserves it by construction
         and needs no supervision. Now stated as the differentiator.

  FOUND  arXiv:2507.08794: LM judges accept substantively empty responses at
         35-67% given a superficial prefix.
  EFFECT Supplies the contrast case: learned verifiers carry large epsilon
         AND are attackable. Motivates the two-rate view empirically.

  FOUND  Selective prediction and LLM cascades are the closest ALGORITHMIC
         relatives of the reject-gated rule.
  EFFECT New related-work paragraph. Differentiator: cascades gate on a
         calibrated confidence and inherit miscalibration; a sound rejection
         is a logical certificate, so the gate needs no calibration, no
         threshold, and no distributional assumption. R1 below must be filled
         with the actual classical citations.

PART 0A — DEEP AUDIT FOR OVERCLAIM AND CORRECTNESS (2026-07-20)
================================================================

Twelve findings. All twelve are fixed in this build. Listed worst first.

A1. FOUR CHECKS WERE PRESENTED AS SOUND AND ARE NOT.  [was in Table 1]
    Soundness is the paper's central concept, and the table asserted that
    "each check is nominally sound because a gold output satisfies it by
    definition of the task." That was false for at least four entries, and
    a gold counterexample exists for each:
      - SQL "non-empty result set": a gold query answering a question whose
        true answer is "none" returns zero rows.
      - SQL "question entities appear in predicates": a gold query can
        reach an entity through a join key without naming it.
      - Morphology "segment count in attested range": a distributional
        prior, not a definitional constraint; rare legitimate forms fall
        outside it.
      - SQL "projected type matches expected answer type": needs an oracle
        for the expected type, which can be wrong on gold.
    Two further entries were sound only under an unstated scope condition:
      - IE "every span is a literal substring" holds only if the task is
        purely extractive, not if it normalises dates, numbers or
        coreference.
      - Morphology "segments concatenate to the surface form" holds only
        for SURFACE segmentation of CONCATENATIVE morphology. It fails for
        canonical segmentation and for templatic morphology. The paper is
        multilingual, so this matters.
    FIX: Table 1 is now split into a sound column and a NOT-sound column
    with the scope conditions italicised in place.

A2. THE UNDERLYING TENSION WAS NOT STATED, AND IT QUALIFIES PROP. 4.
    A1 is not a list of slips. The checks one reaches for to drive epsilon
    down are disproportionately the ones whose soundness argument fails,
    because the property that makes a check discriminative (it rules out
    much of the output space) is the property that makes it liable to rule
    out a gold output. Prop. 4 as written reads as a licence to accumulate
    checks. It is not.
    FIX: new paragraph in Section 5 stating the tension explicitly. It also
    resolves it: by Cor. 1 the discriminative unsound checks belong in the
    SCORER, where their delta costs nothing because the gate has already
    run. The framework says what to do with them.

A3. THE NOVELTY CLAIM AGAINST DORNER ET AL. WAS TOO STRONG.
    The paper claimed the floor "has no analogue" in their analysis. But
    the reject-gated rule IS rejection sampling with the greedy output as
    the first draw, so it is not a new algorithm. Worse, homogeneous
    rejection sampling with a sound verifier already cannot fall below the
    SAMPLED accuracy p, since p/q >= p whenever epsilon <= 1. A floor of
    some kind is not ours.
    FIX: stated plainly. What the gate adds is that the floor sits at p_0
    rather than p, which requires the first draw to be distinguished and
    to have different accuracy from the pool. The two differ exactly when
    greedy beats sampling. Ungated best-of-N has neither floor.
    This narrows the contribution and should be read as narrowing it.

A4. A CLAIM WAS ATTRIBUTED TO THE WRONG PAPER.
    The brittleness taxonomy (formatting, notation, algebraic equivalence)
    was cited to Cai et al., which formalises verifier noise as a reward
    channel. The taxonomy is from TinyV.
    FIX: citation split; each paper now carries only what it argues.

A5. A FACTUAL ERROR ABOUT CONSTRAINED DECODING.
    The paper claimed that "whether segments concatenate to the surface
    form cannot in general be enforced token by token." That is false.
    Concatenation to a KNOWN surface form is a simple prefix constraint: a
    decoder tracks how much of the surface has been consumed and masks
    accordingly. It is one of the easier things to enforce during decoding.
    FIX: the false example is removed. Executability against a live
    database is retained, which is genuinely not prefix-decidable. The
    paper now concedes that where a constraint IS decodable, decoding it
    is the better engineering choice.

A6. "THE CONSTRAINTS WITH THE SMALLEST EPSILON ARE PRECISELY THE ONES THAT
    ARE NOT PREFIX-DECIDABLE." Asserted with no support.
    FIX: demoted to an explicit conjecture that the paper rests nothing on.

A7. "SPECIFICITY DELIVERS ALL OF THE GAIN." False.
    The gain is (1-p_0)(1-eps)*pi with pi = p/q, so the third factor
    depends on candidate quality p as well as on epsilon. At p = 0 the
    gain is zero for every epsilon: a verifier cannot manufacture a
    correct answer the model never samples.
    FIX: the conclusion now says soundness appears in none of the three
    factors and specificity enters two of them, with the p = 0 caveat
    stated.

A8. "STRUCTURAL CHECKS IN NLP ARE SOUND AND UNSPECIFIC." Contradicted by
    the paper's own Section 5 after A1, and by its own nominal/realised
    distinction.
    FIX: now "nominally sound and, we expect, unspecific", with both the
    expectation and the realised-soundness question flagged as open.

A9. "MORPHOLOGICAL CONCATENATION IS THE TIGHTEST SINGLE CHECK IN THE
    PAPER." Asserted as fact; no epsilon has been measured.
    FIX: restated as an expectation that Experiment 2 tests.

A10. "PRECISELY THIS CLASS OF BRITTLENESS." The TinyV categories overlap
    ours without coinciding.
    FIX: the overlap is now stated as partial.

A11. THREE OVERFULL DISPLAY EQUATIONS in the appendix proofs.
    FIX: broken across lines. Build is clean above 15pt.

A12. ABSTRACT/RELATED-WORK INCONSISTENCY created by the A3 fix.
    FIX: the abstract now says a general ROC analysis has "no reason to
    isolate" these properties rather than that it cannot surface them.

WHAT THE AUDIT DID NOT FIND
  The mathematics is correct. All five results were re-derived by hand and
  independently simulated. The exchangeability step in the gain proof holds
  (conditioning on the accepted SET, each accepted candidate is correct
  with probability p/q). The union bound in the stacking proof needs no
  independence. The convexity argument for the heterogeneous case is right,
  and the appendix correctly uses a covariance decomposition rather than
  bare Jensen, which handles p_0 and epsilon co-varying. The q = 0 and
  q' = 0 degenerate cases are handled. No fabricated citation, number, or
  attribution was found beyond A4 and A10; every one of the 13 references
  was checked against a source this pass.

STILL AT RISK, NOT FIXABLE HERE
  Every empirical claim in the paper is a prediction. The audit can confirm
  that predictions are labelled as predictions; it cannot confirm they are
  true. B1 and B2 remain the binding constraints.

PART 0B — DEEP AUDIT OF THE EXPERIMENTS (2026-07-20)
================================================================

Nine findings. All nine are addressed in this build. Worst first.

X1. THE DELTA MEASUREMENT WAS CIRCULAR, AND IT WOULD HAVE VOIDED
    EXPERIMENT 2 FOR THE CHECK THAT MATTERS MOST.
    Experiment 2 measures delta = P[reject | correct]. That needs a
    correctness label C. For text-to-SQL the default label is execution
    match, which marks a query that FAILS TO RUN as incorrect. But
    "executes" is one of the checks being audited. So under execution
    match, the executability check has delta = 0 BY CONSTRUCTION, not by
    measurement, and the soundness audit is vacuous for exactly the check
    the paper leans on hardest.
    General rule, now stated in the paper: a correctness label that
    presupposes a check cannot be used to measure that check's error rate.
    FIX: switched to test-suite accuracy (Zhong, Yu and Klein, EMNLP 2020),
    which labels queries by behaviour across distilled databases and does
    not presuppose one successful execution. A red flag now requires the
    same circularity check for the IE and morphology metrics.

X2. NO BASELINE ISOLATED THE PAPER'S ONLY ALGORITHMIC CLAIM.
    The contribution is the gate. The baseline list contained no rule that
    differed from ours only in the gate, so no result could have attributed
    anything to it. Worse, homogeneous rejection sampling was absent
    entirely, despite being (a) what Dorner et al. show dominates
    best-of-N at fixed compute and (b) what our own rule reduces to when
    the first draw is not distinguished.
    FIX: baselines restructured into three groups with the gate-isolating
    pair first: ungated best-of-N and homogeneous rejection sampling, both
    with the same verifier, scorer and candidate pool. The paper now says
    no result is interpretable without them.

X3. NO EXPERIMENT TESTED THE MECHANISM, ONLY THE OUTCOME.
    FIX: new Experiment 3b. Hold verifier, scorer and pool fixed; vary only
    the rule. Then sweep decoding temperature so that p_0 - p ranges from
    near zero to large. This yields the sharpest falsifiable prediction in
    the paper: the gate's advantage over rejection sampling comes ENTIRELY
    from the base being better than a sample, so as temperature falls and
    p_0 - p goes to zero, the advantage must vanish. If it persists at
    p_0 = p, the mechanism is not what we claim. If no configuration gives
    p_0 > p on these tasks, the gate has no room to help and we say so.

X4. COMPARISONS WERE BUDGET-MATCHED, NOT COMPUTE-MATCHED.
    The gated rule returns at the gate whenever the base is accepted, so at
    a nominal N it draws far fewer candidates than an ungated rule at the
    same N. Reporting both at equal N would have flattered our method, and
    a reviewer would have caught it. Note that GradeSQL flags the same
    issue about its own ORM comparison.
    FIX: all comparisons now reported against realised mean generations per
    instance, not nominal N.

X5. A CURRENT SOTA BASELINE WAS MISSING.
    DPC (arXiv:2604.15163, April 2026) is training-free candidate selection
    for text-to-SQL, frames the identical problem as a generation-selection
    gap, and reports up to 2.2 points over strong selection baselines. It
    was neither cited nor compared against. It is the closest experimental
    neighbour the paper has.
    FIX: added as a baseline and discussed in related work, where it also
    illustrates the paper's own taxonomy: DPC is a strong DISCRIMINATOR
    that is not sound, since a correct query can disagree with an imperfect
    reference implementation, so by Cor. 1 it belongs behind a gate rather
    than in one. AUTHOR LIST FLAGGED: one name rendered oddly in the source
    consulted and must be confirmed against the title page.

X6. THE SELF-CONSISTENCY BASELINE WAS A STRAW MAN.
    For text-to-SQL the standard is to cluster candidates by EXECUTION
    RESULT and return a member of the largest cluster. String-level voting
    over SQL text is much weaker and nobody uses it.
    FIX: specified as execution-clustered self-consistency.

X7. THE CENTRAL PREDICTION IS A NULL, AND THE STATISTICS COULD NOT HAVE
    SUPPORTED IT.
    The paper predicts cheap sound checks buy almost nothing. A
    non-significant difference from an underpowered comparison is absence
    of evidence, not evidence of absence, and every near-null claim in the
    paper would have rested on that error.
    FIX: pre-specified equivalence margin, two one-sided tests for the
    near-null claims, and the minimum detectable effect reported at the
    achieved sample size for every comparison asserting a small gain. Red
    flag added requiring the margin be fixed BEFORE results are seen.

X8. NO CONTAMINATION CHECK. Spider and BIRD are old and public, and p_0 is
    precisely the quantity contamination inflates. A contaminated p_0
    shrinks the measured headroom (1 - p_0) and would make the paper's
    prediction come true for the wrong reason.
    FIX: red flag added requiring a check per benchmark per model, or an
    explicit statement that disclosure is unavailable and the numbers are
    an upper bound.

X9. NO TRAINED SOTA SELECTOR PINNED. The reward-model baseline is cited but
    the current strongest trained selector per benchmark is not fixed.
    FIX: red flag requiring the BIRD and Spider leaderboards be checked at
    submission time WITH THE ACCESS DATE STATED.

WHAT THE EXPERIMENTS STILL DO WELL
  The ordering is right: the cheapest experiment can refute the account,
  and the gates are stated in advance with the refuting outcome named. E1
  is a genuine positive control. E6 is a genuine negative control with a
  quantitative prediction rather than a vibe. E2 measures the quantities
  the theory reasons about rather than a proxy for them. That structure is
  better than most submitted papers have, and it survives this audit.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE PAGE BUDGET
  These additions cost roughly a page. Content now runs to about 9 pages
  against an 8-page limit, BEFORE any result is written. See B5: cutting
  is no longer optional, and Section 6 is where it must come from, since
  the >>> TO RUN blocks collapse to past-tense reporting once run.

THEORY: VERIFIED CORRECT.
  Theorem 1 was re-derived branch by branch and checked at three points.
    N = 0    ->  A_0 = p_0(1-delta) + p_0*delta = p_0.  Correct.
    delta=0  ->  reduces exactly to the gain corollary.  Correct.
    N -> inf ->  reduces exactly to the degradation corollary.  Correct.
  The three terms correspond to: base accepted and correct; base rejected
  and nothing accepted, so the base is returned and is correct only via the
  delta channel; base rejected and something accepted. The decomposition is
  exhaustive and the branches are disjoint. A1 gives independence of the
  samples from the base, which is what licenses the (1-q)^N factor; A2
  gives conditional independence of verdicts, which is what licenses q
  being the same for every candidate. Both are stated in the assumption
  block, neither is buried in a proof.
  No error found in any of the five results.

NOTATION: THREE DEFECTS, ALL FIXED.
  N1. pi appeared exactly ONCE in the paper, in the abstract, in the
      expression (1-p_0)(1-eps)*pi, and was DEFINED NOWHERE. An undefined
      symbol in the abstract is the kind of thing a reader trips on in the
      first fifteen seconds.
      FIX: the abstract now states the three factors in words. pi is gone
      from the paper entirely; the PDF contains no pi character.
  N2. q carried TWO different definitions. The introduction defined
      q = p + (1-p)eps, the sound-case formula. Theorem 1 redefined it as
      q = p(1-delta) + (1-p)eps. The second subsumes the first, but the
      reader meets the narrow one first and is never told.
      FIX: the theorem now says both quantities generalise the ones named
      in the introduction and reduce to them at delta = 0. The q = 0
      degenerate case is also handled in the statement.
  N3. FOUR symbols were in use for closely related replacement quantities:
      pi (= p/q), r (= p(1-delta)/q), rho_N (= (1-(1-q)^N) r), and
      h (= (1-eps) r).
      FIX: pi removed; rho_N now defined THROUGH r so the relationship is
      visible; the introduction states that r and q are the ones that
      persist and rho_N is local to that paragraph.

OVERCLAIM: ONE FOUND, PLUS ONE INTERNAL CONTRADICTION.
  C1. Prediction P3 asserted that morphological concatenation "is the
      tightest single check here" as a fact. The same claim in Section 5
      had already been hedged to an expectation in the previous audit, so
      the paper contradicted itself, and the unhedged version was the one a
      reviewer would quote.
      FIX: P3 now states it as an expectation that Experiment 2 tests.
  C2. P3 also predicted that stacking "the tighter checks of Section 5"
      raises the gain. After the previous audit those tighter checks had
      been moved to the NOT-sound column, so the prediction referred to
      checks the gate no longer contains, and it sat in tension with the
      paper's own finding that discriminative checks tend to be unsound.
      FIX: P3 now names the sound column explicitly, predicts a SMALL
      effect for exactly the reason Section 5 gives, and says a large
      effect would be welcome news about the sound column.
  C3. P3 and P4 had no failure clauses while P1 and P2 did.
      FIX: added. Every prediction now names what its failure would mean.

HALLUCINATION: NONE FOUND THIS PASS.
  No new citation was added since the previous pass except zhong2020testsuite
  and dpc2026, both verified. Every number in the paper is either a
  placeholder, a figure attributed to a source that was read, or an output
  of the released code.

PROSE: ONE REAL TIC, CORRECTED.
  Measured over 278 body sentences: mean 22.4 words, standard deviation
  12.0, range 3 to 74. That variance is human-range; uniform sentence
  length is the usual generated-text signature and this paper does not
  have it. Hollow intensifiers, "delve", "leverage", "underscore",
  "showcase", "nuanced", "plays a crucial role", and the "not only X but
  also Y" construction: zero occurrences of each. No em dashes.
  The one genuine fingerprint was the construction "X rather than Y",
  used 26 times, including twice in a single sentence and twice in
  consecutive sentences on two occasions.
  FIX: 11 instances rewritten as "not Y", "instead of Y", or restructured.
  Now 13, which is ordinary for a paper of this length. Also removed
  "for two reasons we treat as first-class" and one "which is why".

WHAT I STILL CANNOT VERIFY
  That the propositions are the right MODEL of the phenomenon, as opposed
  to internally consistent. A2 assumes delta and eps are identical for the
  greedy output and the samples; that is an idealisation, it is flagged in
  the paper, and Experiment 2 measures both separately. If greedy outputs
  are systematically better formed than sampled ones, the theorem's
  branch-1 term is wrong and every downstream number shifts.
  The DPC author list, one name of which rendered oddly in the source
  consulted and is flagged in the .bib.

METHOD. Three independent checks, not visual inspection:
  1. Text-vs-text: every label bounding box in the figure region tested
     pairwise for intersection, with font padding stripped so the boxes
     approximate glyph ink.
  2. Text-vs-ink: every vector stroke on the page decomposed into segments
     (Bezier curves flattened to 8 segments each) and tested for
     intersection against every label box. This is the check that catches a
     curve running through a caption, which a bbox-vs-bbox test cannot.
  3. Numerical correctness: the plotted coordinates parsed back out of the
     LaTeX source, the axis scaling inverted, and each point compared
     against the governing formula recomputed independently.
